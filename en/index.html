Three ways to work

Test what you know. Learn what is new. Read in between.

Test

A verse is drawn. You carry on from memory.

Choose a surah, then a hizb, a rubʿ, a page or the whole surah. The app opens the real page: one verse, drawn at random, stays clear; the five that follow are blurred.

  • Every word the app hears comes out of the blur at once.
  • Stuck? A hint shows the next word. Nothing is ever counted as a mistake.
  • At the end: the words heard, the hesitations, the verses to review.

Learn

One verse at a time, seven times, then without the text.

The verse is shown large. You recite it seven times while reading it — the ring only moves when the whole verse was heard. Then the text hides, and you recite it from memory.

  • Three levels: the words appear as you say them, then fade behind you, then nothing shows.
  • A reciter can read the verse to you first.
  • Stop whenever you like: the app resumes exactly where you were.

Read

The mushaf, page by page — with a voice, a tafsir and a search.

Turn the pages with a swipe. Touch a verse to listen to it, read its tafsir (al-Muyassar, in Arabic) or see the verses that look like it elsewhere in the Qur’an.

  • Listen to a page: the word being recited is coloured as it is said.
  • Search with Arabic words, a surah’s name, a reference or a page number.
  • One bookmark, and the tajwīd colours if you want them.

An honest tool

It tells you what it heard. Nothing more.

A phone’s microphone can tell which words were said. It cannot judge a ghunnah or a madd reliably enough to correct anyone — so Ihfaz does not pretend to.

What it shows you

  • The words it heard, and the ones it never heard
  • Where you hesitated, and where you took a hint
  • Your pace — when there is enough recitation to measure it
  • The verses that look alike, which so often derail a memory

What it does not claim

  • It does not correct tajwīd
  • It never says “wrong”
  • It does not replace your teacher
  • It shows no number the measurement does not support

Your recitation is yours

The listening happens on your iPhone.

Ihfaz recognises your words with a model trained on Qur’anic recitation that runs on the phone itself. Your voice is not recorded, not kept, and not sent to us.

  • 0recordings kept
  • 0accounts to create
  • 0ads or trackers

One optional engine, offered in the settings as a backup, uses Apple’s speech recognition, which works through Apple’s servers. iOS asks for your permission before that can happen.

Progress

See your memorisation, verse by verse.

Every verse you learn or test is remembered on your phone: what is memorised, what is in progress, what is due for review today.

  • Your memorised verses out of the 6,236 of the Qur’an
  • The words that keep slipping, one touch from their page
  • The thirty juz at a glance
  • A streak, with no pressure

The details that matter

The real page

The Madinah mushaf in fifteen lines, word for word, as you hold it in your hands.

Tajwīd colours

The prolongations, the ghunnah, the qalqalah — coloured as in the printed mushaf, or switched off.

Two reciters

Al-Ḥuṣarī and al-Minshāwī, verse by verse or a whole page.

A real search

Type as you write today, without vowels: the app finds the verse in the mushaf’s own spelling.

Three languages

English, French and Arabic — with the whole interface mirrored for Arabic.

Works without a network

The text, the search and the recognition of your voice need no connection. Only the reciters’ audio is streamed.

Questions people ask

Is it really free?

Yes. Ihfaz is free, with no ads and nothing to buy in this version.

Does it correct my tajwīd?

No, and on purpose. It checks the words you recite, not how they are pronounced: a phone’s microphone cannot do that reliably. For tajwīd, nothing replaces a teacher.

Which recitation does it follow?

Ḥafṣ ʿan ʿĀṣim, on the fifteen-line Madinah mushaf.

Is my voice recorded?

No. The audio is listened to and dropped immediately; nothing is written to a file and nothing is sent to us.

Which iPhones can run it?

Any iPhone with iOS 16.4 or later.
